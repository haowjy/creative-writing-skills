# Reviewers

## `@critic`

Name one lane per critic:

- **A focus area** (structure, character, voice, prose, or continuity) for
  adversarial depth on one dimension. Tell it to load
  `/creative-writing-craft` for a prose or voice focus, and `/shared-dao` for
  vocabulary checks.
- **An edit level** (editorial review, developmental edit, line edit,
  copyedit, or proofreading). Editorial review is the holistic book-editor
  pass: what kind of revision the draft needs, in priority order across
  structure, voice, line quality, and surface. Tell it to load
  `/creative-writing-craft` for editorial review and line edits.

Run focus areas as parallel lanes, scaled to stakes: one or two for a
low-stakes passage, three for a standard chapter, four or five for a pivotal
scene, with the dimension the scene's purpose rides on covered twice. For a pivotal scene or a
disputed judgment, run the same lane on two strong model families and
synthesize where they disagree. Use an editorial-review lane when the draft
needs an order of work across concerns; use focus areas for depth on one.

Brief each round's lanes with the earlier reports on the same chapter, so they
can tell a recurring concern from a new one. Rounds stop at the limit in
`/story-workflow` § Own the Verdict.

## `@continuity-checker`

It reads across the project for deep cross-project validation, and costs more
than a critic with the continuity focus. Use the critic lane for routine
checks.

## `@reader-sim`

Name the reader persona and its knowledge boundary: what this reader has
already read. Tell it to load `/character-sim` when the persona is a specific
character type. Run it after the critique rounds converge, before presenting
to the author: a scene can be technically clean and leave a reader cold.
